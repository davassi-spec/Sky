## Ciutadella through the eyes of a guide: history, walking tour, markets, and local flavors

Ciutadella isn’t just toured: it’s *read*. There are chapters in stone —palaces, temples—, paragraphs that smell of the market, and footnotes that only appear if you go slowly. Here I tell you how I experience it in my tours: with stories, little tricks, and a route designed to savor the city without rushing.

## Walking tour of Ciutadella (approx. 2 hours) — 15 stops with soul

### 1) Plaça des Born · the city’s gateway

An urban hall with a vocation for the stage. The obelisk commemorates 1558; around it, palaces that whisper old surnames. Here I mark the “mental map” to orient ourselves: the old town, the port, and the pace of the tour.

Local tip: return at sunset —the low light and golden stone work magic for photos.

### 2) Ses Voltes Viewpoint

Look out at the arcades: a corridor with shade, shops, and the usual conversations. The echo of footsteps is part of Ciutadella’s character.

### 3) Cathedral of Menorca (Santa Maria)

Sober Gothic on the outside, recollection on the inside. We go in if the schedules allow; if not, I’ll tell you why the temple explains so much about the old ecclesiastical capital.

Cathedral of Menorca. Photo: Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0 ES).

### 4) Torre-Saura Palace

A lesson in stone of what the Menorcan nobility was like. Look at the ironwork and the proportions: elegance without shouting.

Palau de Torre-Saura. Photo: Josep Renalias (Lohen11) / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA).

### 5) Palau Salort and noble surroundings

Portals, shields, and courtyards that tell lineages. During the Festes de Sant Joan, this space vibrates differently; the city has equestrian memory.

### 6) Claustre del Carme

A cloister that offers shade and calm a minute from the hustle and bustle. Ideal for a coffee break.

### 7) Mercat Municipal and Mercat des Peix

The daily pulse: seasonal fruit, freshly caught fish, and bars where you can understand “tapeo” in the Menorcan way. If there’s music or a market, let the route catch on.

Mercat Municipal (Plaça de la Llibertat). Photo: Wikimedia Commons (CC0).

### 8) Carrer Major d’es Born (Ses Voltes)

Arcades, artisans, ovens: a soundtrack of footsteps on stone. Look for the details in carvings and capitals.

### 9) Plaça Nova and artisan neighborhood

Small shops, workshops, and cafes. If you’re traveling with children, it’s a good time for a shared ensaimada.

### 10) Descent to the old port

Suddenly, everything is an amphitheater: terraced houses, docks, and a canal that smells of salt. Here the city sits to look at the sea.

Port de Ciutadella. Photo: Peer / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA).

### 11) Moll de ponent: old houses and boats

A walk next to working boats. If you catch the elders chatting, you’ll hear the sea time better than in the forecast.

### 12) Canal viewpoints

Essential photo stop. Pay attention to the reflections at dusk.

### 13) Old city wall gates

Topography of the walled city: imagining the gates is understanding the movement of people and goods.

### 14) Plaza de la Llibertat (tapas and break)

Arcades and bars with local flavor. If there is a product market —or crafts in summer—, extend the stop.

### 15) Return to the Born

The city returns us to the main stage to close the route. If the sky cooperates, stay and watch how the facades turn golden.

> **Game of hidden details:** look for a “head” carved in stone, a plaque with double toponymy (Catalan/Spanish), and a horseshoe on a door. If you find all three, the city winks at you.

## Markets and crafts: the city that has breakfast in the square

The **Plaça de la Llibertat** is a ritual: coffee under arcades, quick shopping, and a chat. Several mornings a week —with special joy **Tuesdays, Fridays, and Saturdays**— there is movement; in summer, **evening craft markets** usually appear in the center. Schedules change according to the season: check the municipal agenda or ask me, and we’ll adjust the visit.

## Where to eat: classic seafood, market, and signature cuisine

### Sea and tradition

In the old port, you will find temples of fish and caldereta. Book in advance in high season and ask about the product of the day.

### Market that is eaten

In the Llibertat and surroundings, there are bars that serve dishes from the mercat: fast, local, and tasty. Ideal for “snacking” during the route.

### Tapas with identity

Look for bars where the menu speaks Menorcan: Mahón-Menorca cheese, sobrasada, “olives trencades”, Menorcan-style fish.

### Signature cuisine

If you fancy a contemporary twist, book a table in creative proposals in the old town: local product, technique, and careful winery.

Note: confirm schedules and weekly rest (they change by season). If you write to me, I’ll suggest tables according to your route and preferences.

## Festes de Sant Joan: the city that rides

They start with the *Diumenge des Be* and culminate on June 23 and 24. The *Caragol des Born* transforms the square into a collective heartbeat, and the *jocs* on the 24th keep the equestrian tradition alive. It is an exciting and demanding party: book in advance, hydrate, and follow local instructions.
