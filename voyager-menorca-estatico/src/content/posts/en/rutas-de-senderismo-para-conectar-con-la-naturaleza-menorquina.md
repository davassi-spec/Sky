## Hiking trails in Menorca: 6 itineraries to connect with nature

Camí de Cavalls, ravines, caves, and the s’Albufera des Grau Natural Park: real distances, times, tips, and rules to enjoy without risks.

Menorca has been a **Biosphere Reserve** since 1993 and an open-air classroom for those who walk: black rocks of Paleozoic origin in the north, light sandstones in the south, ravines with holm oaks and wild olive trees, and the *s’Albufera des Grau* wetland, the ecological heart of the island. The *Camí de Cavalls*—a historic path that circles the coast in 20 stages—allows you to explore it in sections. Here I propose **six varied routes** with distances, times, and practical tips, plus **basic rules** to conserve this unique environment.

Best time: March–June / Sept.–Nov. · Avoid midday hours in summer · Difficulty: low to medium

## 1) Favàritx → Es Grau (Camí de Cavalls, coastal section)

A geological classic of the north. It starts next to the Favàritx lighthouse, in a dark landscape of slates and clays, and progresses towards light sandy coves and the fishing village of Es Grau. Well-marked coastal path, with small climbs and rocky sections.

## 2) Cala Pilar → Ets Alocs (Tramuntana coast)

Scenic route between **reddish dunes**, cliffs, and pebble coves. It can be done as a *linear* route (Pilar → Ets Alocs) or as a *round trip* from the Camí del Pilar parking lot. Spectacular panoramic views on clear days.

## 3) Binigaus → Cova des Coloms (the “cathedral” of Menorca)

Perfect walk for families. From Binigaus beach or the inland path, the trail enters a ravine with holm oaks and wild olive trees until reaching the enormous Cova des Coloms (110 m long and about 24 m high). **Flashlight** and closed shoes are essential; respect the bats.

## 4) Barranc d’Algendar (Ferreries): shade, orchards, and birds

One of the longest ravines in Menorca. Very pleasant route on hot days: there is **shade** for a good part of the way, birdsong, and traditional farms. Ideal for observing Mediterranean flora (mastic, buckthorn, palmetto) and dry stone walls.

## Tips, safety, and equipment

- **Footwear**: hiking or sports shoes with marked soles. Avoid flip-flops.
- **Water**: minimum 1.5–2 L per person on coastal routes. Add salts on hot days.
- **Sun and wind**: hat, sunglasses, **biodegradable** cream. With tramontana winds, look for routes in the south; with south wind, the north.
- **Navigation**: the Camí de Cavalls is signposted, but take a map/GPX if you deviate. Mobile coverage may fail.
- **Respect**: close gates, do not step on crops, and greet the farmer; the paths cross private farms with right of way.
- **Transportation**: combine bus, taxi, or transfer for linear routes (e.g., Favàritx → Es Grau).

## Conservation rules

- Stay on the **paths** and respect the **dune** and the **posidonia**.
- It is **forbidden** to make fires, camp, or leave trash. Always carry your trash bag.
- **Dogs** on a leash in sensitive areas and outside of beaches with restrictions.
- **Do not collect** flora, fossils, or stones; they are part of the ecosystem.
- **Drones**: prohibited in protected areas unless expressly authorized.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I Do these Routes with Children?

Yes: Binigaus–Cova des Coloms and the s’Albufera itineraries are ideal. Adjust the times and bring water and snacks.

### Is a Guide Necessary?

It is not mandatory, but **a local guide** provides security, shortcuts, environmental interpretation, and logistics (transfers, schedules, alternatives due to wind).

### Can I Swim on all the Routes?

On the coastal routes, yes (Favàritx–Es Grau, Pilar–Ets Alocs, Macarella–Turqueta). In ravines and the natural park, respect wildlife areas and signage.
