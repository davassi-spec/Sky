Maó (Mahón in Spanish) is the capital of Menorca and a city that uniquely blends history, sea and Mediterranean life. Its impressive **natural harbour**—one of the largest in the Mediterranean—has been a strategic hub since Antiquity, attracting Talayotic settlements, Roman merchants, British strategists and travellers from all over the world. ## 🏺 A brief history of Maó: from Talayotic port to capital

### The origins: Talayotics, Carthaginians and Romans

The area around the harbour has been inhabited for thousands of years: on the outskirts of the city, the Talayotic settlement of **Trepucó**—with one of the largest taulas on the island—reminds us that people lived here long before Maó existed. Tradition attributes the city’s name to the Carthaginian general **Mago**, Hannibal’s brother, and with the Roman conquest in **123 BC** the site was consolidated as **Portus Magonis**.

### The British century: the capital that arrived by sea

The big turning point came in the **18th century**. The British, who ruled Menorca in three periods between **1708 and 1802**, quickly understood the harbour’s military value, and in **1722** Governor **Richard Kane** moved the capital from Ciutadella to Maó. From that era we still have the **Camí d’en Kane**, sash windows and *boinders* (bow windows), a taste for gin… and a good share of the anglicisms in Menorcan Catalan.

### Bourgeois and seafaring Maó

During the 19th century, Maó enjoyed its commercial golden age: the fortress of **La Mola** (1852) protected the harbour entrance, the **Teatre Principal** premiered Italian operas, and merchants built the stately homes that still give the centre its character. Today it’s a calm, authentic city, where life still revolves around the market, the squares and the harbour.

## ⚓ The harbour: 5 kilometres of history

Maó harbour is the city’s reason for being: an estuary almost **5 kilometres** long, deep and sheltered, considered one of the **largest natural harbours in the Mediterranean**. Walking along the Moll de Llevant promenade at sunset, with terraces lit up and sailboats coming in, is one of the city’s great pleasures.

Inside the harbour there’s a small archipelago steeped in history:

- **Illa del Rei:** where **Alfonso III landed in 1287** for the Christian conquest. The British built their **naval hospital** here in 1711; today, restored, it also houses an international art gallery.
- **Illa del Llatzeret:** the great late-18th-century quarantine fortress, where crews were quarantined. It can be visited in season.
- **Illa Pinto and Illa de la Quarantena:** former naval bases that complete the ensemble.
- **La Mola (Fortalesa d’Isabel II):** at the northern harbour entrance, an impressive 19th-century defensive complex open to visitors all year round.

**Guide tip:** a **glass-bottom boat** trip around the harbour is the best way to understand Maó: from the water you can read three centuries of military and health history in a single ride.

## 🧭 The old town: squares, markets and viewpoints

### Plaça de la Constitució

Maó’s civic heart, dominated by the **Town Hall**—with its clock brought by Richard Kane—and the church of **Santa Maria**. Its layout reflects the 18th-century urban reorganisation.

### Plaça d’Espanya, Mercat des Peix and the Costa de ses Voltes

The **fish market** (1927) is now also a spot for tapas and vermouth, and the staircase of the **Costa de ses Voltes** connects the upper town with the harbour in one of Maó’s most photographed scenes.

### Es Claustre del Carme

The former neoclassical cloister of the Carme convent houses the **municipal market**: Maó-Menorca cheese, sobrasada, rustic bread and fresh produce under historic arcades. A must-stop to understand the island’s pantry.

### Viewpoints

From the **Pont des Castell**, the viewpoint at Plaça de la Miranda or the des Freginal park, you’ll get the best panoramas of the harbour and its islands.

## 🏛️ Must-see landmarks

- **Church of Santa Maria.** Rebuilt in the 18th century, it holds one of the city’s treasures: the **monumental organ** with more than 3,000 pipes, built by Swiss master Johann Kyburz and installed in **1810**. From May to October, recitals are held almost daily.
- **Teatre Principal (1829).** The **oldest operating opera house in Spain**, with its elegant horseshoe-shaped auditorium designed for acoustics. It still programmes opera, music and theatre all year round.
- **Birthplace of Mateu Orfila.** The Maó-born **Mateu Orfila (1787–1853)** is considered the **father of modern toxicology**; a bust commemorates his legacy.
- **Sant Francesc.** The convent complex that closes off the city to the north, with its Baroque façade and the cloister that now houses the Museu de Menorca.
- **Arco de Sant Roc.** The only remaining vestige of Maó’s medieval wall.

## 🖼️ Museums and culture

- **Museu de Menorca** (Sant Francesc cloister): the island’s main museum, with collections ranging from the Talayotic world to British Menorca. A must if you’re interested in [Talayotic Menorca](/en/menorca-talayotica-guia/).
- **Ca n’Oliver:** a bourgeois mansion with the Hernández Sanz–Hernández Mora collection and magnificent views from its tower.
- **Museu Hernández Sanz and the rooms of Es Claustre del Carme:** exhibitions about Maó in the 18th and 19th centuries.

## 🍷 Where to eat and what to order in Maó

- **Moll de Llevant:** the line of harbourfront restaurants, ideal for fresh fish, rice dishes and seafood stews with views of the boats. [View on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=restaurantes+Moll+de+Llevant+Ma%C3%B3)
- **Mercat des Peix:** seafood tapas and vermouth at the fish market. [View on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Mercat+des+Peix+Ma%C3%B3)
- **Es Claustre del Carme:** local produce to take away or snack on under the arcades. [View on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Claustre+del+Carme+Ma%C3%B3)

### What to order: flavours of Maó

- **Seafood stew and sailors’ rice dishes**, with the harbour right in front of you.
- **Maó-Menorca D.O.P. cheese**, which takes the city’s name from the harbour it was exported from.
- **Mahonesa sauce:** yes, mayonnaise was born here (see curiosities).
- **Xoriguer gin and pomada:** the distillery, a British legacy, is still right on the harbour and can be visited.
- **Ensaïmada, pastissets and cuscussó** for something sweet.

## 💡 Curiosities almost nobody tells you

- **Mayonnaise is from Maó.** According to tradition, after the French capture in 1756, the Duke of Richelieu’s cook took to Paris the “mahonesa sauce” he had discovered on the island.
- **The father of toxicology was from Maó.** Mateu Orfila became dean of the Faculty of Medicine in Paris.
- **Spain’s oldest operating opera house isn’t in Madrid** or Barcelona: it’s the Teatre Principal in Maó (1829).
- **The Town Hall clock was brought by a British governor**, Richard Kane—the same man who moved the capital and built the road that bears his name.
- **The “boinders”**—wooden bay windows—are Menorca’s version of English bow windows.
- **The harbour once hosted the Royal Navy’s Mediterranean base**, and legend places Admiral Nelson at Golden Farm, the red estate overlooking the north shore.

## 🏖️ The surrounding area of Maó

- **Es Castell:** the most British town on the island (the former Georgetown), with the charming Cales Fonts.
- **Trepucó and Talatí de Dalt:** two Talayotic settlements just outside the city.
- **S’Albufera des Grau Natural Park:** the heart of the [Biosphere Reserve](/en/menorca-biosphere-reserve/), 15 minutes away.
- **Favàritx Lighthouse:** Menorca’s most photographed lunar landscape (more in our [lighthouse guide](/en/guia-faros-menorca/)).
- **Binibèquer Vell and the southern beaches of Sant Lluís:** 10–15 minutes by car.

## 🌤️ Climate and average temperature: when to come

Maó shares the island’s Mediterranean climate, with the tramontana a regular winter visitor. Facing east, its **sunrises over the harbour** are as memorable as Ciutadella’s sunsets.

| Month | Avg temp | Sea | Vibe |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | ~11°C | ~14°C | Quiet, ideal for museums and the harbour |
| April | ~14–15°C | ~15°C | Perfect for strolling the old town |
| May | ~17–18°C | ~17°C | Excellent: terraces and harbour cruises |
| June | ~21–22°C | ~22°C | Summer; organ concerts begin |
| July | ~24–25°C | ~25°C | Peak summer; Festes de la Mare de Déu de Gràcia coming up (7–9 Sept) |
| August | ~25°C | ~26°C | The warmest and liveliest month |
| September | ~22–23°C | ~25°C | Unbeatable: Gràcia festivities and warm sea |
| December | ~12°C | ~16°C | Mild; Christmas lights and atmosphere |

**When to come?** For good weather without the crowds, **June and September** (with the **Festes de Gràcia**, from 7 to 9 September, as the finishing touch). For culture and walking, **April, May and October**.

## ℹ️ Practical information for visiting Maó

### How to get there and get around

Menorca **Airport is just 10 minutes** from the centre. Maó is also the island’s main ferry port. The old town is easy to explore **on foot**; for the harbour, bear in mind the hill (or the ses Voltes staircase).

### Where to park

Car parks by the harbour (Moll de Llevant) and in the s’Esplanada area; paid street parking in the centre. In summer, earlier is better.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### How much time do I need to see Maó?

For the historic centre, museums, the market and a walk around the harbour, ideally you’ll want **a full day**. With half a day you can see the main highlights.

### Are there concerts at Santa Maria?

Yes: the **historic organ** offers regular recitals, especially between **May and October**. Check the times at the church.

### Can you visit the harbour islands?

**Illa del Rei** and **Llatzeret** can be visited in season with boat departures from the harbour; **La Mola** is open all year round.

### Maó or Mahón?

Both: **Maó** is the Catalan/Menorcan name and **Mahón** is Spanish. The official name is Maó-Mahón.
